# Economic Model

The economic architecture of the Execution Economy aligns value distribution with measurable contribution.

---

# Revenue Distribution

Enterprise revenue may be distributed across four structural pools.

Execution Pool  
Operations Pool  
Platform Infrastructure Pool  
Founder Pool

The exact proportions vary by industry, but the Execution Pool should represent the largest share.

---

# Execution Units

Contribution is measured using Execution Units (EU).

Examples include:

- completing a delivery
- completing a ride
- completing a service task
- executing an operational action

Execution Units form the base metric for economic participation.

---

# Quality Multipliers

Execution Units are adjusted through quality multipliers.

Factors may include:

- customer ratings
- reliability
- punctuality
- service consistency

Example model:

5 stars → 1.5x  
4 stars → 1.2x  
3 stars → 1.0x  
2 stars → 0.7x

This ensures the system rewards valuable execution rather than raw volume.

---

# Reputation Capital

Network contributions generate reputation capital.

Examples include:

- mentoring new executors
- supporting operational stability
- improving service standards

Reputation capital influences opportunity and trust within the network.

---

# Decay Mechanism

Participation weight gradually decreases when contribution stops.

This ensures that economic influence reflects current participation rather than historical accumulation.

---

# Freeze Conditions

In certain circumstances, decay may be temporarily suspended.

Examples include:

- illness
- injury
- maternity or paternity leave
- exceptional personal hardship

This preserves fairness without encouraging passive accumulation.

---

# Economic Philosophy

Execution-Owned Enterprises aim for **compressed inequality**.

Leadership roles may earn more due to responsibility and risk, but the enterprise avoids extreme disparities that detach leadership from the execution network.

The goal is alignment, sustainability, and shared success.
