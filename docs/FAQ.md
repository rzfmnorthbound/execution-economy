# Frequently Asked Questions

## What is the Execution Economy?

It is a framework for organizing service enterprises where the network of people who perform the work participates in governance, reputation systems and economic value creation. It introduces the concept of an Execution-Owned Enterprise as an alternative to extractive platform models【453533534731398†L40-L58】.

## How does the 51/49 governance split work?

Strategic decisions are governed by a dual authority: 51% of the voting power is held by founders/board and 49% by the execution network【439113443820217†L102-L115】. This ensures that the network has real voice while founders can protect long-term coherence and prevent hostile capture. Operational decisions are led by domain heads.

## Is this a cooperative or a DAO?

No. The model maintains clear leadership (founders and domain heads) while integrating execution networks into governance. It is not a pure democracy and it does not rely on blockchain or token speculation. It is a pragmatic, founder-led enterprise model.

## How do executors earn participation?

Executors accumulate participation through Execution Units (tasks completed), Quality Multipliers (ratings and reliability) and Reputation Capital (contributions to the network)【286127764546600†L49-L67】. Participation is active; it decays if contribution stops.

## How is compensation determined?

Compensation is separated from governance. Economic participation reflects meaningful contribution: Execution Units adjusted by quality and reputation. The enterprise should avoid extreme internal disparities and may distribute revenue among execution, operations, platform and founders.

## What sectors can use this model?

Any service where execution networks drive value: delivery, transportation, logistics, home services, care services, technical field services, distributed operations and more.

## Does this require legal restructuring?

Enterprises adopting this model may need to adapt governance documents and employee agreements to formalize participation rights and reward structures. Laws vary by jurisdiction; consult legal experts when implementing.

## How is this different from gig platforms?

Gig platforms treat workers as independent contractors and capture most of the value. Execution-Owned Enterprises treat executors as structural participants with voice and economic participation【453533534731398†L40-L58】. This creates stronger networks, better service quality and alignment.

## What about investors?

Investors can still participate. Founders and investors maintain a share of revenue and governance. The model encourages investment in infrastructure and network growth, balancing returns with sustainable participation.
